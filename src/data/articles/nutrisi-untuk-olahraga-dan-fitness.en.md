"What should I eat before the gym?" or "can I eat right after training?" sound like simple questions, but the answers matter quite a bit for how much you actually get out of your training effort — whether that's an intense 20-minute EMS session, a breathless HYROX-style circuit, or regular weightlifting. Plenty of myths circulate around this topic, from "never eat carbs before exercise" to "if you don't get protein within 30 minutes after training, your muscles won't grow." This guide summarizes sports nutrition based on the scientific consensus from ISSN and ACSM, two leading sports nutrition references, with practical benchmarks you can apply right away.

## Before Exercise: Fueling Up

The ACSM/ISSN principle: prioritize **easily digestible carbohydrates** plus a small amount of **protein**. If time is tight, cut back on **fat** and high **fiber** — both slow gastric emptying, risking discomfort during intense movement.

- **2-3 hours before**: a full, balanced meal — complex carbs, protein, a bit of fat, vegetables (rice + chicken/fish with minimal oil + boiled/lightly stir-fried vegetables), giving enough time for digestion so your stomach isn't full when training starts
- **1-2 hours before**: a smaller, simpler portion — carbs + a bit of protein, without much fat
- **30-60 minutes before**: a simple, low-fat/low-fiber carb snack that digests quickly — banana, dates, young coconut water, whole-grain crackers, or bread with honey

If you train in the morning and don't have time for a full meal, a small high-carb snack is still better than training on a completely empty stomach, especially for high-intensity sessions.

## After Exercise: The Recovery Phase

After training, the body is in a state that needs repair: muscle glycogen stores are depleted, and there's micro-damage to muscle fibers from repeated contractions, especially with weightlifting, EMS, or high-intensity circuits. A combination of **protein + carbohydrates** is ideal: protein supplies amino acids for muscle protein synthesis, while carbs help refill glycogen and support amino acid delivery to muscle tissue.

### The "Anabolic Window" Myth of 30 Minutes

An ISSN position stand (Kerksick et al., 2017, JISSN) concludes that the post-workout nutrient window is much wider than 30 minutes — it can stretch for hours depending on when you last ate. What matters far more for long-term results is **total daily protein intake and consistency**, not how fast you drink a protein shake after training.

> The post-workout anabolic window is much wider than the 30 minutes often talked about.

Example foods: rice with chicken/fish/egg + vegetables, whole-grain bread with egg, a protein shake blended with banana, or braised tempeh/tofu for a plant-based option.

## How Much Protein Do You Need?

For people who exercise regularly, especially with weightlifting or high-intensity training like EMS and HYROX-style workouts, protein needs are higher than for inactive people. An ISSN position stand (Jäger et al., 2017) recommends **1.6-2.2 grams/kg body weight/day** to support recovery, maintain muscle mass, and facilitate the body's adaptation to training load — the higher end of the range applies during a calorie deficit. Spreading it across **3-4 meals** (~0.3-0.4 g/kg per meal, e.g., ~21-28 grams per meal for a 70 kg person) appears more effective at stimulating muscle protein synthesis than stacking nearly all your protein into 1-2 large meals.

## Hydration: Don't Wait Until You're Thirsty

Fluid needs for people who exercise regularly are much higher than the general "8 glasses a day" guideline, especially in Indonesia's hot, humid tropical climate. A practical benchmark: pale-yellow urine indicates good hydration, while darker urine means you need more fluids. For prolonged high-intensity sessions (HYROX-style) or hot weather, consider an electrolyte drink. *(Full details in the separate article "Hydration: Daily Fluid Needs and Its Impact on Exercise Performance".)*

## Supplements: Proven vs. Just Hype

**Strongly supported by evidence:** whey/plant protein powder (not a magic supplement, but a practical way to hit your daily protein target, especially for people who struggle to eat enough protein from whole food due to time constraints); creatine monohydrate (one of the most researched and consistently effective supplements for strength, explosive power, and performance, typical dose 3-5 grams/day taken regularly); caffeine (helps focus, lowers perceived fatigue, modestly boosts performance when taken 30-60 minutes before a session — the response is individual, and some people are sensitive to its effect on sleep).

**Worth being skeptical about:** BCAAs (if your daily protein intake from whole food or protein powder already covers the 1.6-2.2 g/kg range, adding separate BCAAs generally offers no meaningful extra benefit, since complete protein sources already contain BCAAs); fat burners (most products have weak/inconsistent evidence, and none can replace the role of a calorie deficit and consistent training in fat loss).

> Supplement means "addition", not replacement. A whole-food-based diet with enough calories and protein is always the main foundation.

## Nutrition for EMS and HYROX-Style Training

Electrical muscle stimulation (EMS) and HYROX-style training have somewhat different characteristics from conventional exercise. EMS triggers very intense muscle contractions in a relatively short time — despite the short duration, the micro-damage to muscle fibers and the recovery demand can be substantial, with effects similar to heavy weightlifting. This means adequate protein intake, ideally at the upper end of the 1.6-2.2 g/kg range, is important to support muscle tissue repair. HYROX-style training, which combines running with functional strength stations, draws on two energy systems at once — endurance and strength — so having enough carbohydrate available before and during training is important for keeping glycogen stores up so performance doesn't drop sharply in the final stations. The combination of high intensity, longer duration, and Indonesia's hot, humid weather means sweat losses can be greater — paying attention to fluid intake before, during, and after training is an important part of keeping performance steady.

## Bottom Line

Effective sports nutrition isn't as complicated as it's often made out to be: easily digestible carbs before training, a protein+carb combo afterward, total protein of 1.6-2.2 g/kg spread evenly through the day, hydration matched to your body and the weather, plus creatine and protein powder when needed. Beyond that, day-to-day consistency is what matters most.
